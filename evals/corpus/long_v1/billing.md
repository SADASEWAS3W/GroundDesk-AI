# Subscriptions and Invoices Handbook

## Separate plan selection from invoice review

A subscription plan describes the capabilities available under a subscription. An invoice records charges for a billing period. These are related topics, but changing a plan and investigating an invoice are different operations. Start by deciding whether the question is about choosing a plan, when a change takes effect, locating an invoice, or disputing a charge shown on a document.

The fictional platform's documented plans are Free, Pro at $29 per month, and Enterprise with custom pricing. The listed Pro amount describes the plan in the seed documentation. When reviewing an actual invoice, also read its billing period, per-seat charges, add-on costs, and taxes rather than assuming a single plan label explains every line item. This handbook expands the seed example and does not establish a new pricing policy.

## Compare the documented capabilities

The Free plan includes up to 3 projects and 5 team members. Pro provides unlimited projects, advanced analytics, and priority support. Enterprise adds single sign-on, audit logs, and a dedicated account manager. These are the capabilities listed in the plan comparison. They should be kept distinct from invoice fields, which describe how a particular bill is presented.

For example, a question about project capacity can be answered from the Free and Pro comparison, while a question about audit logs belongs to the Enterprise description. Read the named capability together with its plan. Avoid assuming that a feature appears on every plan merely because it is mentioned elsewhere in the same handbook.

## Request a subscription change

Upgrade or downgrade from Settings > Billing. Changes take effect at the start of the next billing cycle. The documented ability to request a change at any time does not mean the new plan takes effect immediately. These two statements answer different questions: where and when a change can be requested, and when the requested change becomes effective.

When preparing a billing question, note the current plan, the requested plan, and the billing period you are examining. This is an organizational suggestion for explaining the situation. It does not change the effective-date rule or imply an additional credit, refund, trial, or exception. The supported timing answer remains the start of the next billing cycle.

## Read the invoice as a billing-period record

Invoices are generated on the first of each month and sent to the billing email on file. The generation date describes the monthly invoice workflow. It should not be confused with the timing of an arbitrary plan-change request. Locating an invoice starts with the billing email and the invoice history, not with changing the account's subscription merely to make a document appear.

Each invoice shows the plan name, billing period, per-seat charges, add-on costs, applicable taxes, and total amount. Review the period before comparing two invoices. Then inspect the charge categories shown on each document. This is a reading sequence for the documented fields, not a new formula for computing taxes or a promise that every account has the same add-ons.

## Retrieve past documents and report a discrepancy

Download past invoices from Settings > Billing > Invoice History. This path is for previously generated billing documents. It is different from the general billing page used to request a plan upgrade or downgrade, although both are within the Billing area. Identify the relevant billing period when choosing a past invoice for review.

If an invoice contains an error, contact the billing team within 30 days. Describe the field or charge that appears incorrect and the invoice period involved. Reporting an invoice discrepancy is distinct from requesting a subscription change. The documented contact window should not be interpreted as a refund policy or a guarantee of a specific resolution.

Before sending a question, distinguish the evidence you have from the result you are requesting. For example, an unexpected total is an observation; a suspected tax error is an issue for review. Sharing the relevant invoice reference and a concise description helps explain the concern without inventing an entitlement that the underlying plan and invoice articles do not define.
